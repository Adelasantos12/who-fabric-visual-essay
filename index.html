A visual data essay · WHO Financing Networks · 2016–2025

The Fabric of
Global Health Funding

Every global health programme is connected to its funders through a thread of money. When many actors fund many programmes, a fabric forms. Whether that fabric holds together — or quietly comes apart — is a structural question, not a financial one.

This essay maps nine years of WHO financing data as a changing network. The story is not about the total amount of money. It is about the texture of the connections.

Data: WHO Programme Budget (Q4 2016–2023; Q1 2024–2025)  ·  Analysis: Santos Dominguez et al. (peer-reviewed)  ·  Network: igraph (R)  ·  Visualization: D3.js

Scene 01 — The unit

What is a thread?

Each financing relationship in the WHO system connects one actor — a government, a foundation, an international agency — to one WHO programme or recipient country through a flow of money.

In network terms: the actor is a node, the money is an edge, and the amount is the weight. When many nodes send many weighted edges to many recipients, a fabric forms.

The question this essay asks is not how much money flowed — but whether the resulting fabric held together.

Scene 02 — 2016–17 Baseline

A relatively integrated fabric

In 2016–2017, the WHO financing network shows its most integrated structure in the observed period. Density is modest — the fabric is not tight — but it is coherent: relatively few disconnected pieces, moderate clustering, and the lowest modularity of any biennium.

Nodes: 636  ·  Edges: 3,477
Density: 0.009  ·  Clustering: 0.043
Weakly connected components: 2
Modularity: 0.294

Two weakly connected components means the network is essentially one integrated structure with one small satellite. The fabric holds — not tightly, but coherently.

The US cluster of entities (USAID, CDC, NIH and others) accounts for 616M — 12.4% of total positive flows. Hover over nodes to see individual actors.

Scene 03 — 2018–19: Early drift

The patches separate — before any shock

Two years later, and before any major external disruption, the network shows a striking structural shift. The number of weakly connected components grows from 2 to 123. Modularity rises by 59%.

WCC: 2 → 123
Modularity: 0.294 → 0.466 (+59%)
Density: 0.009 → 0.008 (slight decline)

The fabric is not broken — but the patches are becoming more self-contained. Actors are funding within communities rather than across them. This pattern is suggestive of declining integrative capacity.

Scene 04 — 2020–21: Expansion without binding

More knots. More threads. Less fabric.

During the COVID-19 period, the network expands dramatically — 60% more actors, 36% more financial links. And every measure of structural cohesion deteriorates simultaneously.

Nodes: 624 → 999 (+60%)
Edges: 3,277 → 4,444 (+36%)
Density: 0.008 → 0.004 (−50%)
Clustering: 0.050 → 0.012 (−76%)
WCC: 123 → 546 (+344%)

The fabric expands and thins simultaneously. New emergency actors enter. Existing ties proliferate. But the structure loses its reinforcing patches and becomes 546 loosely connected pieces.

Scene 05 — 2022–25: Partial recovery

The fabric partly re-stitches — but not to its original texture

Post-pandemic, the network contracts and partially reorganizes. Density and average degree recover — more threads per knot. But clustering and weakly connected components do not return to 2016–17 levels.

Avg. degree: 4.45 → 7.69 (highest observed)
Density: 0.013 (above 2016–17 baseline of 0.009)
Clustering: 0.014 (vs. 0.043 in 2016–17)
WCC: 121 (vs. 2 in 2016–17)

2024–25*: density 0.012 · WCC 126 · clustering 0.018
* Q1 2025 only — provisional

The recovery is real but structurally incomplete. The new equilibrium is consistent with a different configuration — not a return to the pre-shock basin.

2025: What happens when a thick thread thins?

US entities — USAID, CDC, NIH, DTRA, USFDA, USDA, State Department — contributed 25M in the 2024–25 period (provisional): 10.9% of total positive flows, distributed across 1,530 financing relationships.

The scenario below fades US-linked flows to show which parts of the network become more exposed. Grey edges = US flows being removed. Highlighted nodes = those receiving ≥50% of their flows from US entities.

25M removed · 1,530 edges faded · 4 nodes with ≥50% US dependency highlighted
Top 35 donors + WHO recipients · 2024–25 (Q1, provisional)

Finding: No node in this dataset receives flows exclusively from US entities — the network has no single point of US-only dependency at this aggregation level. Four recipient nodes rely on US sources for 50–74% of their observed flows: Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, and Iraq.

Middle Powers: The Potential Bridges?

As the US presence in the network fluctuates, other actors have the scale to act as structural bridges. Germany, the European Commission, Canada, and major partnerships like GAVI have the volume and geographic reach that could position them as brokers.

Middle-power candidates (gold stroke)

However, structural betweenness remains low across the entire network (all values < 0.001). While these actors are large funders, none yet occupies a dominant brokerage position in structural terms. Their role as "bridges" is a potentiality, not yet a confirmed structural reality.
